Order this first

Ayam sereh

Javanese fried chicken. We marinate it overnight in a spice paste heavy on lemongrass, dip it in a cornflour batter and fry it, then serve it with balado, our chilli paste.

Want it gentle? Ask, and we'll keep the balado on the side. Most of the menu can be made without chilli.

Since 1984

We started as a takeaway counter for students a long way from home.

In 1984 most of the people at our counter were Indonesian students from UNSW, up the road, looking for food that tasted like home.

Today you'll find us on Avoca Street, in the quieter part of Randwick. Wawan Kurniawan has run the kitchen for more than thirty years and still does the cooking. Her son Johannes looks after the dining room.

The menu runs to seven pages and travels the whole archipelago, from Javanese fried chicken to Balinese grilled chicken and rendang with clove and star anise. Some of the families eating here tonight have been coming for three generations.

How to eat at Java

  1. Order for the tableA few dishes in the middle, rice for everyone, and keep passing.
  2. Tell us about chilliMost dishes can be made non-spicy. Say so when you order.
  3. Leave roomSticky-rice pudding, durian ice cream or es bumi hangus.
  4. Stay for fruitEvery meal ends with fruit and biscuits, on the house.
